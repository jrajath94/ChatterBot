# Founder Philosophy Rubric — Distilled From Primary Sources

Compiled 2026-05-23 by research agent #3 (swarm of 6).
Purpose: a concrete rubric the solo founder can score any candidate idea against before May 25 deadline.
Sources: primary essays/letters by Paul Graham, Jeff Bezos, Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Warren Buffett / Charlie Munger.

## PART 1 — Per-Thinker Distillation (concrete heuristics, primary-source quotes)

### 1. Paul Graham (paulgraham.com)

**A. From "How to Get Startup Ideas" (startupideas.html)**
1. *Live-in-the-future test.* "Live in the future and build what seems interesting." Ideas should feel **noticed**, not invented.
2. *Organic vs made-up.* The good ones come from "noticing something missing in your own life." Made-up ideas are "plausibly but suspiciously appealing."
3. *Well, not a shallow hole.* "Build something a small number of people want a large amount."
4. *Crappy V1 test.* "Who wants this so much that they'll use it even when it's a crappy version one made by a two-person startup they've never heard of?"
5. *Obviousness test.* "When you find the right sort of problem, you should probably be able to describe it as obvious, at least to you."
6. *Path-out test.* Niche start is fine, but there must be a credible expansion (Facebook expanding from Harvard outward).
7. *Schlep filter / unsexy filter.* Strip away aversion to tedium and aesthetic dismissal — many valuable ideas hide here.

**B. From "Do Things That Don't Scale" (ds.html)**
- "You have to go out and get them" — recruit users by hand (Stripe's Collison installation; Wufoo's handwritten notes).
- "The feedback you get from engaging directly with your earliest users will be the best you ever get."
- Use your own software *on behalf of* users (Viaweb hand-built stores).
- "It's not the product that should be insanely great, but the experience of being your user."

**C. From "Schlep Blindness" (schlep.html)**
- Definition: "Your unconscious won't even let you see ideas that involve painful schleps."
- Reframing trick: stop asking "what problem should I solve?" and ask **"what problem do I wish someone else would solve for me?"** — bypasses the blind spot.
- Stripe is the canonical example: payments were obviously valuable; nobody worked on them for a decade because the schlep (banks, fraud, regulation) repelled hackers.

**D. From "Novelty and Heresy" (nov.html)**
- "To discover new things, you have to work on ideas that are good but non-obvious; if an idea is obviously good, other people are probably already working on it."
- "Every cherished mistaken assumption has a dead zone of unexplored ideas around it."
- "If you're looking for new ideas, one way to find them is by looking for heresies."
- Search-tool form: find a reasonable domain expert proposing something that sounds wrong, then ask questions instead of dismissing.

**E. From "Frighteningly Ambitious Startup Ideas" (ambitious.html)**
- 7 named: new search engine, replace email, replace universities, internet drama, the next Steve Jobs, bring back Moore's Law, ongoing diagnosis.
- Method: **don't telegraph.** "Just say you're building todo-list software... People can notice you've replaced email when it's a *fait accompli.*"
- "The way to do really big things seems to be to start with deceptively small things."
- "It may be better to have a blurry [vision of the future]."

**F. From "Startup = Growth" (growth.html)**
- "A startup is a company designed to grow fast."
- Weekly growth: 5–7% solid, 10% exceptional, 1% means PMF not found.
- "If you don't know that number, you don't even know if you're doing well or badly."
- "Optimizing for growth... can actually discover startup ideas." Growth as evolutionary pressure on the idea itself.

**G. From "Black Swan Farming" (swan.html)**
- Best startup ideas live in Thiel's Venn intersection of "sounds bad" ∩ "is good."
- "In startups, the big winners are big to a degree that violates our expectations about variation."
- At YC, two companies (Dropbox + Airbnb) drove ~3/4 of value. **Outliers dominate.**
- Founder implication: dismissive ≠ wrong. Optimize for "right kind of crazy."

**H. From "The Hardware Renaissance" (hw.html)**
- Hardware is viable now because of crowdfunding, online distribution, prototyping tools, accessible manufacturing.
- "Investors have a deep-seated bias against hardware" — but founders see further than investors.
- Heuristic: where logistics align with desire, hardware startups can succeed.

**PG synthesis (5 PG questions to ask the idea):**
1. Did you notice this, or invent it?
2. Would *you* use the crappy V1?
3. Is there a painful schlep at the core that's repelling other founders?
4. What heresy does it require believing?
5. Could you measure 5–10%/week growth in 6 months?

### 2. Jeff Bezos (Amazon shareholder letters, primary text)

**A. 1997 letter — the founding tenets**
- "We believe that a fundamental measure of our success will be the shareholder value we create over the long term."
- "From the beginning, our focus has been on offering our customers compelling value."
- "We will make bold rather than timid investment decisions where we see a sufficient probability of gaining market leadership advantages."
- "When forced to choose between optimizing the appearance of our GAAP accounting and maximizing the present value of future cash flows, we'll take the cash flows."
- "Setting the bar high in our approach to hiring has been... the single most important element of Amazon.com's success."

**B. 2015 letter — Type 1 vs Type 2 decisions**
- "Some decisions are consequential and irreversible or nearly irreversible — one-way doors — and these decisions must be made methodically, carefully, slowly, with great deliberation and consultation." (**Type 1**)
- "Most decisions aren't like that — they are changeable, reversible — they're two-way doors. If you've made a suboptimal Type 2 decision, you don't have to live with the consequences for that long." (**Type 2**)
- Failure mode: using "the heavy-weight Type 1 decision-making process on most decisions, including many Type 2 decisions. The end result of this is slowness, unthoughtful risk aversion, failure to experiment sufficiently, and consequently diminished invention."

**C. 2016 letter — Day 1 / Day 2**
- "Day 2 is stasis. Followed by irrelevance. Followed by excruciating, painful decline."
- Four Day-1 defenses: **customer obsession, skeptical view of proxies, eager adoption of external trends, high-velocity decision making.**
- "Customers are always beautifully, wonderfully dissatisfied, even when they report being happy."
- Resist proxies: "The process becomes the proxy for the result you want. You stop looking at outcomes."
- High-velocity decisions: "If you wait for 90%, in most cases, you're probably being slow." + "disagree and commit."

**D. 2020 letter — distinctiveness**
- "The world will always try to make Amazon more typical — to bring us into equilibrium with our environment." Distinctiveness requires active work.
- "Invention is the root of all real value creation."
- "Be kind, be original, create more than you consume, and never, never, never let the universe smooth you into your surroundings."

**Bezos synthesis (6 questions):**
1. Are you obsessing over customers, or over competitors / your own metrics?
2. Is this a Type 1 (one-way) decision? If not, decide in days, not months.
3. Are you riding an "eager-to-adopt" external trend? (For 2026: AI, agents, software-for-agents.)
4. Are you trading short-term cosmetics for long-term cash flow?
5. Is there a *distinctive* angle, or are you being smoothed into the environment?
6. Are you making bold investment decisions where the probability of leadership is sufficient?

### 3. Sam Altman (Startup Playbook + "How to be Successful" + 2024–25 AI economics posts)

**A. From "Startup Playbook"**
- "Make something users love" — and "a small number of users love" beats many who like.
- The four pillars (idea+market, team, product, execution) — all four required.
- "Growth solves all problems, and lack of growth cannot be fixed any other way."
- "I have never, not once, seen a slow-moving founder be really successful." Speed + focus.
- Do unscalable things; resist hiring until PMF; "the best ideas sound bad."

**B. From "How to be Successful"** (13 points, condensed)
1. Compound yourself.
2. Have almost too much self-belief.
3. Learn to think independently.
4. Get good at "sales."
5. Make it easy to take risks ("most people overestimate risk and underestimate reward").
6. Focus.
7. Work hard.
8. Be bold — "it's easier to do a hard startup than an easy startup."
9. Be willful.
10. Be hard to compete with (leverage via relationships, brand, unique skill combinations).
11. Build a network.
12. Own things (equity beats salary).
13. Be internally driven.

**C. From "What I Wish Someone Had Told Me" (2024)**
- "Plans should be measured in decades, execution should be measured in weeks."
- "It is easier for a team to do a hard thing that really matters than to do an easy thing that doesn't really matter."
- "Inspiration is perishable... Inaction is a particularly insidious type of risk."
- Avoid fighting fundamental business physics.
- Concentrate resources on a small number of high-conviction bets.

**D. From "Three Observations" + "The Intelligence Age" (2024–25)**
- Cost of a given level of AI falls ~10× / 12 months (150× from GPT-4 → GPT-4o in 18 months).
- Linear intelligence gains → super-exponential socioeconomic value.
- Gains will be "widely distributed" — winner-take-all not guaranteed; agents act as virtual coworkers.
- "The price of many goods will eventually fall dramatically" while inherently limited resources (land, luxury, trust) "may rise even more dramatically."

**Altman synthesis (5 questions):**
1. Do users *love* it (not like)?
2. Can you sustain 5–7%/week growth or PMF in 6–12 months?
3. Are you betting *on* the AI cost curve, or being *killed* by it? (Anything that gets 10× cheaper / year for incumbents to replicate kills you. Anything that *unlocks* at 10×/year cheaper is your tailwind.)
4. Hard-startup paradox: is this hard enough to attract talent and capital, or so easy 1,000 others are doing it?
5. Are you willing to compound for 10 years on this?

### 4. Elon Musk

**A. The Algorithm (5 steps, from Isaacson 2023)**
1. **Question every requirement.** "Make the requirements less dumb." Requirements from smart people are most dangerous, because people are less likely to question them.
2. **Delete any part or process you can.** "If you do not end up adding back at least 10% of them, then you didn't delete enough."
3. **Simplify and optimize.** Only after deletion — simplifying something that shouldn't exist is waste.
4. **Accelerate cycle time.** Only after the first three.
5. **Automate.** Comes last.

**B. First-Principles Thinking**
- "I tend to approach things from a physics framework. Physics teaches you to reason from first principles rather than by analogy."
- Battery example: industry quoted $600/kWh as a "fact"; raw material composition of the cell (cobalt, nickel, aluminum, carbon, polymers, steel can) priced on London Metal Exchange = ~$80/kWh. Stop reasoning from "this is how the industry does it."

**C. Tesla Master Plan (2006) — stepping stones, not frontal assault**
1. Build a (low-volume) sports car. *(Roadster, 2008)*
2. Use that money to build an affordable car. *(Model S, 2012)*
3. Use that money to build an even more affordable car. *(Model 3, 2017)*
4. While doing the above, also provide zero-emission electric power generation options.

The structure exactly matches PG's "frighteningly ambitious → start with deceptively small things." It's the same idea in different vocabulary.

**Musk synthesis (5 questions):**
1. What requirements in this industry are *dumb* — accepted because "that's how it's done"?
2. What would the cost/structure look like if rebuilt from raw materials / first principles?
3. Can you sketch a 3-step Master Plan? (Beachhead → expansion → endgame.)
4. What 10% of your scope can you delete?
5. After deletion, can you accelerate the cycle time of the core loop dramatically (10×)?

### 5. Warren Buffett / Charlie Munger

**A. Circle of Competence (Buffett, 1996 letter)**
- "You only have to be able to evaluate companies within your circle of competence."
- "The size of that circle is not very important; knowing its boundaries, however, is vital."

**B. Durable Moats (Buffett, 1996 + Berkshire usage)**
- "We favor businesses and industries unlikely to experience major change."
- "We are searching for operations that we believe are virtually certain to possess enormous competitive strength ten or twenty years from now."
- Moat types: network effects, switching costs, cost advantage, intangible assets (brand, patents, license), efficient scale.

**C. The Four Filters (Munger)**
1. Is the business **understandable**?
2. Are the **moats** durable?
3. Is **management** able and trustworthy?
4. Is the **price** sensible?

**D. Aptitude / overconfidence (Munger)**
- "You have to figure out what your own aptitudes are. If you play games where other [competitors excel] and you don't, [failure becomes nearly certain]."

**Translation to founder context (the founder's Four Filters):**
1. **Understandable** → Do *I* understand the problem at the operator level (lived it, shadowed it, worked in it)?
2. **Durable** → Will the demand still exist in 10 years? Is the customer-pain root-cause structural, or temporary regulatory/tech artifact?
3. **Management** → Am *I* a great operator of this specific kind of business? (Solo dev + agents + this domain.)
4. **Price** → Is the *entry price* (capital, time, founder-years) right? Or am I overpaying in opportunity cost?

**Munger/Buffett tension warning:** Buffett would tell a founder "avoid Day-1 AI startups, there are no moats yet." That's the wrong frame for an early founder — but the *spirit* (build something with a 10-year demand curve and structural advantage) is the right frame.

**Buffett/Munger synthesis (4 questions):**
1. Inside my circle of competence?
2. Will demand still be here in 10 years?
3. Where is the moat going to come from (network effects, switching costs, data flywheel, brand, regulatory license, distribution, proprietary process)?
4. What's the *real* entry price (founder-years × opportunity cost)?

## PART 2 — Cross-Thinker Contradictions and How to Navigate Them

| Tension | Pole A | Pole B | How a solo dev should reconcile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scale vs unscalable | PG: do things that don't scale. | Bezos: automate, resist proxies, machine learning everywhere. | **PG wins at month 0–6; Bezos wins at month 6+.** Start hand-crafted; instrument every step so you can automate later. With agent swarms, you can compress this faster — but don't skip the manual phase or you won't know what to automate. |
| Moats first vs growth first | Buffett: 10-year moat, then invest. | PG/Altman: grow first, moats emerge with scale. | At the **idea-selection stage**, demand the *theory* of where the moat will come from (data, network, distribution, switching cost). At the **execution stage**, optimize for growth and let the moat compound. Don't pre-build moat infrastructure that has no users. |
| Ambitious vs small | PG: frighteningly ambitious. | PG: start with deceptively small things. Musk: 3-step Master Plan. | **Both:** ambition in the 10-year vision, modesty in the 6-month wedge. The wedge must be a credible step toward the vision (Roadster → Model 3, Harvard → Facebook). |
| Heresy vs understandable | PG: heresy as search. | Buffett: only invest in what you understand. | The heresy must be **inside your circle of competence**. You disagree with the industry not because you're contrarian-for-fun but because you know something operators know and outsiders don't. This is the "outsider+insider" advantage the YC RFS notes flag. |
| Speed vs deliberation | Bezos: 70% info, "disagree and commit," high velocity. | Buffett: long-term, patient, durable. | Use Bezos's Type 1 / Type 2 split. Idea selection = Type 1, takes weeks. Tactical execution = Type 2, takes hours. Solo + agents means you can do 10× more Type 2 cycles, which is the actual unfair advantage. |
| AI bet | Altman: AI 10× cheaper / year, ride it. | Buffett: ignore commodities, find moat. | If the *AI itself* is your product, you're a commodity. If the AI is your tool to build *something with proprietary data, distribution, workflow integration, or trust*, the curve helps you. Test: does GPT-5/Claude-5 obsolete me or empower me? |
| Founder vs investor view | PG/Bezos: founders see further than investors. | Buffett: discipline, fair price, don't overpay. | YC application stage: trust your insider view of the problem; price is set ($200k uncapped SAFE etc.). You're not "overpaying" — you're spending founder-years. The Buffett price filter applies as: *is this the best use of my next 5–10 years?* |

## PART 3 — THE UNIFIED RUBRIC (20 questions)

Score each 0–3. Score interpretation at bottom.

### Origin & insight (PG + Altman + Buffett's understandability)
1. **Noticed, not invented?** Did this idea come from a real frustration I or someone I shadowed lived? (PG-organic) — 0/1/2/3
2. **Inside my circle of competence?** Have I worked in / next to / built in this domain for 1000+ hours? (Munger) — 0/1/2/3
3. **Schlep-heavy?** Does it require boring, tedious, painful work most founders flinch from? (PG-schlep — *good*) — 0/1/2/3
4. **Right-kind-of-crazy?** Does it sound bad to smart people you respect, but the reasons they give don't survive cross-examination? (PG-swan + heresy) — 0/1/2/3

### Demand & users (PG + Altman + Bezos)
5. **Crappy-V1 user exists?** Can I name 5 specific people who would use a crappy V1 from a solo dev? (PG-V1) — 0/1/2/3
6. **Well not shallow hole?** Will these users pay/use *a lot*, not a little? (PG-well) — 0/1/2/3
7. **Customer pain or builder fantasy?** Are users currently doing the painful manual workaround right now? (Bezos-customer-obsession) — 0/1/2/3
8. **Lovable not likable?** Will users *love* the V1, not just like? (Altman) — 0/1/2/3

### Vision & ambition (PG + Musk + Bezos)
9. **Frighteningly ambitious endpoint?** Is there a 10-year version that, if you nail it, is a $10B+ outcome? (PG-ambitious + Bezos-bold) — 0/1/2/3
10. **Master Plan stepping stones?** Can I draw a 3-step plan from wedge → expansion → endgame? (Musk + PG-ambitious) — 0/1/2/3
11. **Heretical assumption?** What 1–2 sentence "everyone believes X but I believe ¬X" thesis am I betting on? (PG-heresy) — 0/1/2/3

### Defensibility & durability (Buffett + Munger + Altman)
12. **10-year demand?** Will this problem still exist in 2036, regardless of who solves it? (Buffett-durable) — 0/1/2/3
13. **Moat theory?** Where will defensibility come from? (Pick at least one: data flywheel, network effects, switching cost, distribution, regulatory, brand, proprietary process.) — 0/1/2/3
14. **AI-curve safe?** When GPT-6 / Claude-6 ships, am I *more* valuable or *obsolete*? (Altman cost curve) — 0/1/2/3

### Execution fit (Bezos + Musk + Altman + solo-dev reality)
15. **Solo-dev tractable?** Can I, with agent swarms, ship a usable V1 in 60–90 days? (Solo reality + PG-V1) — 0/1/2/3
16. **Growth measurable?** Can I instrument a single weekly growth number from week 1? (PG/Altman growth) — 0/1/2/3
17. **Type 1 vs Type 2 clarity?** Do I know which decisions on this idea are irreversible and which are cheap to reverse? (Bezos) — 0/1/2/3
18. **First-principles attack surface?** Is there a "make the requirements less dumb" angle — something everyone accepts as fixed cost / fixed step that isn't? (Musk algorithm step 1) — 0/1/2/3

### Founder fit (Altman + Munger + PG)
19. **10-year obsession?** Could I work on this for a decade without losing interest? (Altman compound + Buffett durable) — 0/1/2/3
20. **Compounding loop?** Is there a system in the business that gets monotonically better the longer it runs (data, brand, network, content)? (Bezos invention + Munger autocatalysis) — 0/1/2/3

**Scoring:**
- **50–60:** strong YC application; submit.
- **40–49:** real idea but a couple of weak filters — write down the weak ones and decide if they're patchable.
- **30–39:** probably re-shape the idea before applying.
- **<30:** wrong idea or wrong founder for the idea.

## PART 5 — Solo Dev + Agent Swarm Lens (where this all lands for the user)

Given the user is a **solo developer** building with **agent swarms**, with a 2-day deadline:

**Bezos's automation lens applied to the founder themselves:** the user *is* the founder + the org chart. Agent swarms are the Bezos-2016 "high-velocity decision making" and the Musk-step-5 "automate" — but they only work if **the user has lived the problem** (PG-organic, Munger-circle), so the agents are scripted to act in a domain the user *already* understands well enough to verify.

**Anti-pattern (PG-schlep-blindness applied):** picking the AI idea that's most fun to build (a wrapper, a model-routing thing, a generic agent platform). These score low on schlep, low on heresy, low on moat — they fail filters 3, 11, 13, 14.

**Pro-pattern:** pick the boring industry the user has worked in, where Excel is the mission-critical software (per YC RFS guide), where there's a schlep nobody wants to do, where you can ship a crappy V1 in 60 days with agents, and where the data you collect from V1 use is the moat. This satisfies PG (organic, schlep, V1, well), Bezos (long-term cash flow, customer obsession), Altman (hard startup, AI tailwind), Musk (delete dumb requirements in an old industry), Buffett (durable demand + emergent data moat).

**One contradiction the user must own:** Buffett says wait for a clear moat; PG/Altman say growth creates the moat. The user's resolution: the moat must be *plausible at idea selection*, *not yet built*. State it explicitly on the YC application: "the moat will be X (data / network / workflow integration / regulatory), which compounds with users, starting at user #50."
