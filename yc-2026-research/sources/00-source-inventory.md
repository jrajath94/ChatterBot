## C. Paul Graham essays (excerpts in screenshots)

### C1. "How to Get Startup Ideas"
- Don't try to think of ideas. Look for problems — preferably your own.
- Best ideas share 3 traits: founders themselves want it, can build it, few others realize it's worth doing.
- Common failure: invent a model of the world that doesn't match reality (PG's 1995 art gallery startup).

### C2. "Novelty and Heresy"
- New ideas hide in dead zones around cherished mistaken assumptions.
- "If you're looking for new ideas, one way to find them is by looking for heresies."
- The more preposterous the assumption, the bigger the dead zone of unexplored ideas around it.

### C3. Earlier YC tweet (9/13/25): "ideas that seem crazy, but the right kind of crazy"
- Good new ideas must seem bad to most people or someone would already be working on them.
- Right-kind-of-crazy ideas are exciting and rich in implications (vs merely-bad which are depressing).

## E. "How to get AI startup ideas according to YC" (image #9)
1. Don't start with "AI ideas". Avoid "ChatGPT for X". Get a real job in industry to disrupt. Find work being outsourced overseas. Study where expensive consultants make too much money. Find places using Excel as mission-critical software.
2. Go deep, not wide. Shadow professionals for a full day. Live the problem. Find pain points nobody talks about publicly.
3. Think in unfair advantages. Mine past jobs. Use outsider+insider perspective. Internet community data is overlooked.
4. Signals: tasks taking hours that should take minutes; problems costing $100k+ annually; manual data entry; industries "stuck in 1995".
5. What works: partner with insiders early; start with small specific user groups; focus on problems not tech; build for revenue not users.
6. Good news: previously-too-small markets now viable; no huge teams needed; 6-12 months to PMF; best opportunities still in boring industries.

## F. Garry Tan on agentic coding (image #10)
- Skill files = reusable markdown docs encoding process not content.
- Harness antipattern: 40+ tool defs eating context window; opinionated narrow tooling wins.
- Resolvers = routing tables for context.
- **Latent vs deterministic distinction**: judgment/synthesis/pattern-recognition → latent; SQL/arithmetic/combinatorial → deterministic. Forcing deterministic problems through models produces plausible wrong outputs.
- Diarization — model reads everything about a subject and produces structured single-page brief. Tan uses this for YC Startup School to match 6k founder profiles nightly and surface gaps between what founders say vs commit history.
